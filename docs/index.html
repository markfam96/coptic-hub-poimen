The weight of shepherding

You carry them all.
Every single one.

A Coptic priest doesn't have 10 spiritual children. He has 150. Some seasons, 200. Each one carries their own burdens — a struggling marriage, a faith that's growing cold, a sin that keeps returning.

He remembers faces. He prays for names. But the details — the canon he assigned in January, the prayer request shared in confidence, the person who hasn't come to confession since Lent — those are harder to hold.

He relies on a notebook. His memory. The rare moment someone reaches out first. It is not enough — not because he doesn't care, but because no human mind was built to shepherd at this scale without a tool.

What gets missed today

✝

The drifting child

Someone confesses a deep struggle in February. By May they've stopped coming. The priest finds out months later — or never. There was no signal, no alert, no way to know.

📜

The forgotten canon

You assigned 40 days of the Agpeya at the end of confession. You have no way to follow up. The canon lives in a notebook and dies there.

◇

The unheard prayer

A spiritual child shares a private prayer request — a sick parent, a marriage in trouble. Between confessions there is no channel. No way to follow up.



Introducing Nepsis


No one falls
through the cracks.

Nepsis is a pastoral care companion — not a productivity app, not a CRM, not a generic tool repurposed for ministry. Every feature was designed around one relationship — the Father of Confession and his spiritual child — and the servant who walks alongside them.

It keeps the Coptic calendar, knows the fasts, teaches the Agpeya by heart, and treats the seal of confession as something to be protected in code — not just in a policy.

"The Good Shepherd leaves the 99 to find the one. Nepsis tells him which one."

Flock Dashboard

Your flock,
at a glance.

  • Confession cadence — Recent under 30 days, Due at 30, Overdue at 60 or with no confession on record
  • Days since last confession on every row — or "No record" when there is none — from the encounters you log and the dates members record themselves
  • Pastoral visit requests surface as a badge, a count, and a filter

Member Detail & Encounter Log

The full picture
of every relationship.

Tap a member for four tabs. Overview holds the spiritual vitals they have agreed to share — nine areas of their rule, measured from what they actually kept — their psalm memorization progress, and their confession history: the dates you logged, the dates they recorded themselves, and any note you chose to leave them. Canon shows the rule they keep and what you assigned. Prayer holds the requests they shared with you. Notes is yours alone.

Log any encounter — a confession, a counseling session, spiritual advice, a home or hospital visit, a call, a group — and leave a note the member will see in their Pastoral Journey. Your own observations belong in the Notes tab: private pastoral notes, encrypted on your device before they are stored.

  • Six encounter types, each dated, with an optional note the member will see in their Pastoral Journey
  • Confession history is dates and type, plus any note you chose to leave the member — there is no field designated for what was confessed
  • Private pastoral notes, encrypted on your phone and readable by no one else — not the member, not the server
  • Tap to call, email, or open directions once a member has shared their contact details with you
  • Add a photo for a member who hasn't set their own — it appears only in your Flock and member views, never in theirs

Canon Builder

Set the rule.
Watch it take root.

A canon is a remedy, not a task list — so it is built the way a father of confession thinks about it. Open a member's canon and it is already filled with the rule they keep today. Switch on the parts you want to set: prostrations, quiet time, the hour to fast until, Bible reading, a spiritual book, confession frequency, the Agpeya hours for each day of the week, church services and Holy Communion, and Heart of Service commitments. Add anything else as free text with its own rhythm.

What you assign is locked for the member until their next confession. Then it folds into their own rule — theirs to keep, and theirs to adjust with you next time.

  • Nine categories, each with an Assign switch — untouched categories stay exactly as the member set them
  • Services by weekday, or by count — Divine Liturgy twice a week, Holy Communion once a month
  • Custom practices with a frequency from daily to twice a year, pinned to the weekdays you choose
  • Save any canon as a named template; apply it to another member in one tap, or undo with a second
  • A Canon Builder workshop for shaping templates with no member attached — Lent, New believer, Youth

Appointments & Scheduling

Open your door
without opening your calendar.

Choose how your flock books you. Take requests inside Nepsis — define your own appointment types, open recurring weekly windows, confirm each request with a tap — or point members to your Calendly page and keep a single source of truth. One path or the other, never both, so no time is ever offered twice.

In-app bookings can be written straight into a calendar on your phone. Anything that reads that calendar — a shared parish calendar, a Calendly you keep for other work — sees the time as taken. Members only ever see anonymized busy times, never who booked.

  • Your own appointment types, from 15 to 90 minutes, each switched on or off
  • Weekly windows per type; close a travel day, part of a day, or open extra hours on a single date
  • A requests inbox with Confirm and Decline — confirming one auto-declines anything that overlaps it
  • A master switch to close scheduling entirely when a season calls for it
  • Members request whatever you offer — a confession, a pastoral visit — from their own Appointments screen

The Sunday School Servant

Sunday didn't end
on Sunday.

You gave your students a Gospel chapter to read each morning. A prayer to say each evening. The lesson shouldn't wait a week to continue.

Every servant has a six-character invite code. A student enters it once in their own app and appears on your roster. From there you keep a record of the practices you have given each child, your private notes from Sunday, and the prayer requests they chose to share with you — all in the same app they already use for their own spiritual life.

  • Student roster — every child who linked to your code, searchable by name
  • Prayer and Scripture practices tailored to each student: ten presets, or your own words
  • Scoped access — the servant portal has no confession history, no encounter log, no counseling records
  • Private notes per student, encrypted on your device and visible to no one else
  • Remove a practice when its season is complete

Walking With Each Student

Know your class
by heart.

Each student has a page of their own. Record the practice you gave them — the Morning Agpeya, a Gospel chapter, a memory verse each week — with a frequency and a start date. Keep the notes you would otherwise lose between Sundays. Read the prayer requests a child decided to share with you, and only those.

When a student shares their spiritual vitals, you also see their psalm streak and how many passages they hold by heart — and when a student opts in, Nepsis shows them a friendly leaderboard of the classmates who opted in too, first name and last initial only.

  • Ten presets in two groups — Prayer and Scripture — or type a custom practice
  • Daily, three times a week, weekly, or a rhythm in your own words, with a start date
  • Psalm memorization progress, shown only when the student has switched on sharing their vitals
  • A class psalm leaderboard that is opt-in, off by default, and never borrows another consent
  • Shared prayer requests decrypted on your device — private and priest-only requests never reach you

Home

Your day,
in the Church's time.

Nepsis opens on the Coptic date beside the civil one, the fast you are in and which day of it this is, and, on days that carry one, the feast of the Lord or the saint remembered that day — all computed on the phone from the Coptic calendar, no connection required.

Two tiles are always pinned: the days since your last confession, measured against the confession rhythm in your rule, and how much of today's canon is done. Below them, your Spiritual Vitals: nine areas of your rule — eight as a percentage of what you have actually kept, and confession as the days since your last — over all time, or from the day you chose to reset them after a confession.

  • Coptic date, fasting season and day count, today's feast or Synaxarium commemoration
  • Spiritual Vitals computed from your own canon — daily prayer, quiet time, Scripture, spiritual book, liturgical services, Holy Communion, fasting, service, confession
  • Pastoral Journey — your most recent encounters with your father, each with any note he left you, alongside the confession dates you recorded yourself
  • Upcoming feasts and commemorations, and a shortcut to your Father of Confession
  • Show or hide sections to make the dashboard your own

Your Spiritual Canon

Your rule,
kept by you.

Set your rule with your Father of Confession, and Nepsis turns it into today's list: the Agpeya hours for this weekday, your prostrations, your quiet time, your reading, the services you committed to. What he assigned carries a lock and stays as he set it until your next confession. The rest is yours to adjust whenever you like.

The app already knows the Church's calendar. On Wednesdays and Fridays outside the Holy Fifty, and on the weekdays of every fasting season, your fast appears on its own and your prostrations rest on the days the Church rests them. Missed a day? Step back up to a week and complete it, so a canon kept but logged late never counts as a miss — only a service counted by the week or month settles once its period has closed.

  • Services by weekday, or by count — "Attend Divine Liturgy — 1 of 2 this week," "Receive Holy Communion — 1 of 1 this month"
  • Log a liturgy, communion, or any other service on any day of the past week, even one your rule didn't ask for
  • Postpone a Heart of Service commitment to next week or next month when life interrupts
  • Communion Readiness — where you stand against your own confession rhythm
  • Your day-by-day check-offs stay on your phone; your father sees a percentage per area, and only if you choose to share your vitals. The one exception is a free-text practice he added himself, which he can see you complete

Confession

Come to confession
prepared.

Between confessions, the journal is where you note what happened as it happens — filed under one of the senses, or one of the four relationships — and when you choose a sin from the Church's catalogue, its description and a verse sit beside it. The examination of conscience walks you through either path: by the senses, forty-three items across the tongue, thoughts, hearing, the eyes, actions, and neglected practices; or twenty-three questions toward God, others, self, and omissions. And when there is no sin in mind, only a question, there is a place for that too.

In the room, open your notes. Everything you marked is there, grouped and ready — your examination, your journal, the questions you want to ask, even the journal entries you flagged to bring up with Abouna. Tap each one as you speak it. When you are done, the screen says what the Church says: Glory to God.

"If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."

1 John 1:9 — as the completion screen greets you

  • Two examination styles: forty-three items by the senses, or twenty-three questions toward God and others — each marked once, a few times, or often
  • A confession journal with the Church's catalogue of sins and the Scripture that answers each
  • A space to ask for guidance — a decision, a struggle, a season of life
  • Everything encrypted on your phone with a key that never leaves it; never sent to a server, never visible to your priest
  • Only the date reaches your Father of Confession — and you can log past confessions, or request a time for your next one, from the same screen

Psalms of the Agpeya

Hide the Agpeya
in your heart.

Every psalm of the canonical hours, and the prayers around them — each hour's Gospel, litanies and absolution, the fixed prayers of the First Hour, the three watches of Midnight, the Prayer of the Veil — 167 passages, ready to be learned by heart. Choose what to memorize and the order to learn it in.

Each passage is broken into short portions. A card shows what came before, then the portion with its ending blanked. You recite, reveal, and grade yourself: wrong, hard, good, or easy. Spaced repetition brings each portion back just as you are about to forget it, one passage at a time, and no portion is ever allowed to outrun the one before it. When every portion of a psalm has stretched to three weeks between reviews, the psalm itself returns — recited whole, from memory.

  • 167 passages in 1,737 portions, grouped by the eight hours of the Agpeya
  • Fill-in-the-blank cards with lead-up context, then whole-passage recitation once a psalm matures
  • A daily streak, your best streak, and a freeze that forgives one missed day a week
  • Seven milestones, from First Fruits to a hundred-day streak and twenty passages held by heart
  • Progress backed up to your account. Your streak and counts reach your father or servant only if you share your vitals — and your classmates only if you join the class leaderboard

Spiritual Journal & Prayer

A private place
to tend your soul.

The journal is yours. Write what moved you in the Liturgy, the verse that spoke to you, what you are bringing to God in prayer today. Mark an entry as a blessing — something wonderful God is doing — and find every blessing again with one tap when a harder season comes. Flag an entry to bring up with Abouna, and it will be waiting in your confession notes. Each day opens with a prompt drawn from the Gospel of the day in the Coptic lectionary — or a fixed verse to sit with when the reading can't be fetched.

Prayer requests reach exactly who you choose: only you, your Father of Confession, your Sunday School servant, or both. The details are encrypted on your phone before they are stored; the server holds only scrambled text, and the keys live on your device and on the devices of the people you chose.

  • Journal entries with a reflection, a Scripture, and a prayer intention — visible to you alone, never to your priest
  • Blessings, marked and gathered, to look back on
  • Prayer requests in seven categories, private by default, end-to-end encrypted
  • Mark a prayer answered; it leaves your father's view the moment you do
  • Your Pastoral Journey on Home — what your priest shared back, in his words

Appointments

Ask for a time
without the back-and-forth.

See the hours your Father of Confession has opened — for confession, for a home visit, for whatever he offers — a year ahead, with taken times already gone. Pick one, add a word about why, and send. It appears under My Appointments straight away as pending, and turns Confirmed when he accepts from his side.

If your father prefers Calendly, the same screen simply shows his booking link. And when a visit is what you need but no hours are open, a single tap on your Home screen lets him know you'd like one.

  • Open times grouped by month and day, filtered by appointment type
  • Request a confession from the Confession screen, or a pastoral visit from your father's card on Home
  • Pending or confirmed status at a glance; cancel to free the time
  • Request Pastoral Visit — a standing flag your father sees on his flock, cleared after he logs the visit

Privacy is a theological commitment.

The holy seal of confession is absolute. Nepsis was designed from the ground up to honor it — not as a legal compliance matter, but as a matter of faith.

🔒

Confession notes never leave your phone

Your examination, journal, and questions are saved on your own device so they are waiting when you go — encrypted with a key that never leaves the phone. They are never sent to a server and never visible to your priest. Delete them whenever you choose.

✝

Only the date reaches your priest

Whether you finish a confession in the app or your priest logs one, what is recorded is who, the date, and the type — plus any note he chooses to write to you, and any private note he keeps for himself, encrypted on his own phone. There is no field anywhere in Nepsis for what was confessed.

◈

Sharing is deliberate

Prayer requests are private by default and end-to-end encrypted. Spiritual vitals and psalm progress are shown to your Father of Confession only after you say yes, and you can switch that off at any time. The class leaderboard has its own opt-in. Your journal is never shown to anyone.

⊕

Locked on the device

On your phone, Nepsis asks for Face ID or your fingerprint every time you return to it — on by default, and yours to switch off — so a phone left on the table is not an open book. Priests, servants, and congregants each see exactly what their role requires — and nothing more.

"What you bring to God stays between you and God. Nepsis exists to support that relationship — not to observe it."
